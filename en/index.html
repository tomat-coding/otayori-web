Four things that make Peragoyomi different

  • Read on your phone

From paper to plan in three steps

Snap it, check it, done. Nothing is added until you've looked at it.

  1. 秋の遠足のお知らせ

    保護者の皆様へ　下記のとおり遠足を行います。

    日時　10月23日（金）8:50 現地集合

    場所　中央公園

    持ち物　お弁当・水筒・レジャーシート

    同意書を10月20日（火）までに担任へ提出してください。

    1Snap it

    Take a photo, or pick photos or PDFs. The camera finds the edges of the page and straightens it. Handouts that arrive by email go straight in from the Share button.

  2. 2Check it

    Everything Peragoyomi finds is shown first. Anything unclear, like a hard-to-read date or a weekday that doesn't match, comes first with a one-tap fix. Only what you tick is added.

  3. 3It's on the calendar

    Items go on the family calendar with a reminder the evening before, and the original photo is kept so you can look back at it.

Search & ask

iPhoneAndroidMac

Type 水筒 (water bottle) or 上履き (indoor shoes) and the handouts and plans that mention it appear as you type. Ask "What do we need tomorrow?" or "What time do we meet for the trip?" and Peragoyomi answers from your schedule and your handouts. Type "dentist tomorrow at 3pm" and it drafts the item for you to confirm.

  • Matches appear as you type: handouts, with the line that matched, and plans
  • Answers link to the items and handouts they came from
  • Works on iPhone, Android and Mac
  • It all runs on your device: nothing you type is sent anywhere

Handouts keep their Japanese, so search them with the Japanese word. Search works the same on every device. On iPhone (iOS 26 or later) and Mac (macOS 26 or later) with Apple Intelligence, you can ask in your own words; on other devices, answers come as quick lists from your schedule and handouts. AI answers can be wrong, so check the handout too.

Search by title or by any word in the handout, and zoom into the original whenever you need it.

  • To do and Key points are picked out on your device, in the handout's own Japanese. Tap one to see it in the original
  • Jump from any item back to its handout
  • Multi-page PDFs stay together as one handout

Handouts sent straight from teachers

On the wayiPhoneAndroidMac

Give a teacher or a lessons class a link, and they can send handouts (PDFs or photos) straight to the app. They open the link in any browser and choose the file. Nothing to install, no sign-up.

  • Handouts arrive on Home, with the teacher's name and message
  • Read them into the usual check screen, and add them once you've checked
  • A link and QR code for each sender: school, nursery, lessons
  • Stop a link any time you no longer need it

Anyone with a link can send through it, so give links only to people you trust. Making a link needs an account. Files are deleted from our server once you've received them, or after 14 days. Whether a teacher can send files this way depends on the school's or nursery's own rules.

Your family's information stays your family's

Handouts are read on your device, and the photos aren't sent anywhere to be read.

  • No account needed

    On iPhone and Android, read handouts, keep your schedule and get reminders without signing in. Signed out, your handouts and schedule stay on your device only.

  • Sign in only to share

    You sign in only to share with family or to use the Mac. Your schedule is then stored on servers in Tokyo, encrypted in transit and at rest. Handout photos aren't shared.

  • No tracking

    Nothing in the app tracks what you do, and there are no analytics tools. On Android, Google's ML Kit, which does the scanning and reading, sends Google diagnostics, but never your photos or their text.

  • Delete everything, any time

    From Settings, delete the data on your device, or your account and all its data.

Questions from parents

Is Peragoyomi free?

Yes. During early access, every feature is free on iPhone, Android and Mac. After the official release, some features may become paid. We're still deciding which ones and the price, and we'll announce it on this page before anything changes.

Will there be ads?

Not during early access. What happens after the official release is still being decided, and we'll announce it on this page before anything changes.

What does early access mean?

You can use the app before its official release. Every feature is free, but it's still being built, so some parts may be unstable and some features may change.

Can it read vertical Japanese handouts?

Yes, on iPhone with iOS 26 or later and Mac with macOS 26 or later. Earlier versions can't read vertical text, so you'd add those items by hand.

Does it handle school payments and cancelled events?

Yes. For payments (集金), it adds the amount and how to pay (bank debit, cash or transfer) when the handout says, and reminds you the evening before. Photograph a notice that cancels or moves an event, and it finds the item you already have and offers to move or delete it. Nothing changes until you confirm.

Does it work for nursery and kindergarten handouts?

Yes. It's built for handouts from schools and nurseries, and you can add a class for each child.

Are my photos or schedule sent anywhere?

Handouts are read on your device, so the photos and their text aren't sent anywhere to be read. If you sign in and share with family, your schedule is stored on servers in Tokyo. On Android, Google's ML Kit, which does the scanning and reading, sends Google diagnostics.

What happens to my data when I change phones?

If you're signed in, sign in with the same account on the new phone and your children, schedule, ticks and handout list come back. Handout photos don't move, and reminder times are kept per phone, so set them again. If you're not signed in, everything lives only on the phone. On iPhone, moving to a new iPhone or restoring from an iCloud or computer backup brings everything across, photos included. On Android, a direct phone-to-phone transfer (Android 12 or later) keeps it, but restoring from a Google Drive backup doesn't.

Do I need an account?

Not on iPhone or Android. You only need to sign in to share with family, sync your own devices, or use the Mac app. Use an Apple or Google account on iPhone, and a Google account on Android. The Mac app signs in when you scan its QR code with your signed-in phone.

How many people can share one family?

During early access, up to 6 people can join one family. iPhones and Android phones can mix, and each person sets their own reminder times on their own phone.

Can grandparents who don't use the app see the plans?

Yes. Send to family turns a handout's to-dos or tomorrow's plan into a message you can paste into LINE or Messages. Whoever gets it needs no app and no account. If you'd like them to see the shared calendar itself, invite them to your family.

Are there limits on storage or questions?

Not during early access. Search & ask has no limit, and handouts have no app-side limit either: you can keep as many as your device can hold.

Which devices can use Search & ask?

iPhone, Android and Mac. Search works the same everywhere. With Apple Intelligence (iPhone on iOS 26 or later, Mac on macOS 26 or later) you can ask in your own words; on other devices, answers come as quick lists from your schedule and handouts. Either way, nothing you type or ask leaves the device.

Does it work on a Windows PC?

A Windows version is on the way. It's the same app as on the Mac: handouts are read on the PC itself, with the text recognition built into Windows. Windows has no on-device AI, so Ask answers with quick lists from your schedule and handouts. For now, the computer app is for Mac (macOS 14 or later), and you start it by scanning a QR code with your signed-in phone. We'll announce it on this page when Windows is ready.

Does it translate handouts into English?

No. The app comes in Japanese and English, but items keep the Japanese wording from the handout.
