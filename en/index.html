Four things that make Otayorin different

  • Read on your phone

    Handouts are read on your phone or Mac itself. The photos aren't sent anywhere to be read.

  • Vertical text, too

    Reads vertical (縦書き) handouts on iPhone with iOS 26 or later, and on Mac with macOS 26 or later.

  • Ask, free on every device

    Ask "What do we need tomorrow?" and get the answer from your handouts, with no limit, on any device.

  • Shared with your partner

    Both of you see the same schedule, even with an iPhone and an Android phone. Sharing is free.

    1Snap it

    Take a photo, or pick photos or PDFs. Handouts that arrive by email go straight in from the Share button.

  2. 2Check it

    Everything Otayorin finds is shown first. Anything unclear, like a hard-to-read date or a weekday that doesn't match, comes first with a one-tap fix. Only what you tick is added.

  3. 3It's on the calendar

    Items go on the family calendar with a reminder the evening before, and the original photo is kept so you can look back at it.

It knows how school handouts are written

Japanese school and nursery handouts use the same phrases again and again. Otayorin turns each one into the right kind of item. Items keep the handout's Japanese wording: there's no translation.

Ask about your handouts

iPhoneAndroidMac

Ask "What do we need tomorrow?" or "What time do we meet for the trip?" and Otayorin answers from your schedule and your handouts. Type "Add gym clothes for Friday" and it drafts the item for you to confirm.

  • Free on every device, with no limit
  • Answers link to the items and handouts they came from
  • Answers are made on your device, and questions aren't sent anywhere

On iPhone (iOS 26 or later) and Mac (macOS 26 or later) with Apple Intelligence, you can ask in your own words. On other devices, answers come as quick lists from your schedule and handouts. AI answers can be wrong, so check the handout too.

Handouts sent straight from teachers

On the wayiPhoneAndroidMac

Give a teacher or a lessons class a link, and they can send handouts (PDFs or photos) straight to the app. They open the link in any browser and choose the file. Nothing to install, no sign-up.

  • Handouts arrive on Home, with the teacher's name and message
  • Read them into the usual check screen, and add them once you've checked
  • A link and QR code for each sender: school, nursery, lessons
  • Stop a link any time you no longer need it

Anyone with a link can send through it, so give links only to people you trust. Making a link needs an account. Files are deleted from our server once you've received them, or after 14 days. Whether a teacher can send files this way depends on the school's or nursery's own rules.

Your family's information stays your family's

Handouts are read on your device, and the photos aren't sent anywhere to be read.

  • No account needed

    On iPhone and Android, read handouts, keep your schedule and get reminders without signing in. Signed out, your handouts and schedule stay on your device only.

  • Sign in only to share

    You sign in only to share with family or to use the Mac. Your schedule is then stored on servers in Tokyo, encrypted in transit and at rest. Handout photos aren't shared.

  • No tracking

    Nothing in the app tracks what you do, and there are no analytics tools. On Android, Google's ML Kit, which does the reading, sends Google diagnostics, but never your photos or their text.

  • Delete everything, any time

    From Settings, delete the data on your device, or your account and all its data.

Questions from parents

Is Otayorin free?

Yes. During early access, every feature is free on iPhone, Android and Mac. After the official release, reading handouts, the calendar, reminders, Ask about your handouts and sharing the schedule with your partner are planned to stay free.

Which features will become paid?

After the official release, sharing with more family than your partner (grandparents or a babysitter, say), reading multi-page PDFs and scans, widgets on iPhone, sharing handout photos with family, more teacher links and more handouts from teachers each month, and the Mac app are planned for the paid plan, おたよりプラス (Otayori Plus). Prices and details will be announced once they're decided.

Will there be ads?

Not during early access. After the official release, the free plan may show ads. おたよりプラス (Otayori Plus) is planned to stay ad-free.

What does early access mean?

You can use the app before its official release. Every feature is free, but it's still being built, so some parts may be unstable and some features may change.

Can it read vertical Japanese handouts?

Yes, on iPhone with iOS 26 or later and Mac with macOS 26 or later. Earlier versions can't read vertical text, so you'd add those items by hand.

Does it handle school payments and cancelled events?

Yes. For payments (集金), it adds the amount and how to pay (bank debit, cash or transfer) when the handout says, and reminds you the evening before. Photograph a notice that cancels or moves an event, and it finds the item you already have and offers to move or delete it. Nothing changes until you confirm.

Does it work for nursery and kindergarten handouts?

Yes. It's built for handouts from schools and nurseries, and you can add a class for each child.

Are my photos or schedule sent anywhere?

Handouts are read on your device, so the photos and their text aren't sent anywhere to be read. If you sign in and share with family, your schedule is stored on servers in Tokyo. On Android, Google's ML Kit, which does the reading, sends Google diagnostics.

Do I need an account?

Not on iPhone or Android. You only need to sign in to share with family, sync your own devices, or use the Mac app. Use an Apple or Google account on iPhone, and a Google account on Android. The Mac app signs in when you scan its QR code with your signed-in phone.

How many people can share one family?

During early access, up to 6 people can join one family. After the official release, sharing with your partner stays free, and adding more family, such as grandparents, is planned for おたよりプラス (Otayori Plus). iPhones and Android phones can mix, and each person sets their own reminder times on their own phone.

Are there limits on storage or questions?

Ask about your handouts is free on every plan, with no limit. Handouts have no app-side limit either: you can keep as many as your device can hold.

Which devices can use Ask about your handouts?

iPhone, Android and Mac. With Apple Intelligence (iPhone on iOS 26 or later, Mac on macOS 26 or later) you can ask in your own words; on other devices, answers come as quick lists from your schedule and handouts. Both are free, and what you ask never leaves the device.

Does it translate handouts into English?

No. The app comes in Japanese and English, but items keep the Japanese wording from the handout.
